02 / What the pilot says

Specific words are useful.
They are still claims.

Brewhouse states when quiet hours apply. Ambassador describes its linens with a quality adjective. Best Western’s room text promises quiet and rest without a concrete noise provision.

That is the distinction this pilot can observe. It cannot tell us which room is quieter, which mattress suits a guest, or whether anyone slept better. Two incomplete sites also prevent a fair comparison of disclosure across all five hotels.

Under a looser rule that treats bedding adjectives as specifics, the specific-cell count would change from one to two. The fixed rubric requires more detail. Read the sensitivity note ↗

03 / How this was done

A small study, with visible edges.

01

Fix the question first.

A purposive 30-name Milwaukee candidate panel and a five-domain rubric were committed before coding. The pilot uses its first five alphabetically named entries. Discovery began before the protocol lock; this is not a preregistered study.

02

Read a bounded set.

Up to four official URLs per property: overview, room details and amenities or FAQs. The 13 URL records include failures. Reviews, booking prices and photo inference are excluded. Extracted text may be cached.

03

Keep unknowns intact.

Each property gets five cells. Inaccessible detail stays separate from “not stated.” Bed size alone is not bedding specificity; room-type inventory alone is not sleep-specific guest choice.

04

Leave room for correction.

One AI-assisted initial coder; no independent reliability estimate. Official addresses were located, but municipal eligibility remains to be checked. The other 25 candidates have not been collected.
