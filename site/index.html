Sleep Is the Product.

A pilot audit of sleep-related information on 5 hotel websites.

What concrete information appears in a bounded review of official hotel pages about noise, light, temperature, bedding and guest requests?

Methods and limitations

A bounded disclosure audit

Selection

The first 5 alphabetically named entries in a fixed purposive candidate panel were reviewed. The panel is not representative; municipal eligibility and current operation remain unverified. The larger panel is a planning target, not a sample-size justification.

Collection

Up to four official URLs per property; 13 URL records include failed attempts. Extracted text may be cached. Dynamic panels were incompletely exposed. Short source notes cannot reconstruct the full negative search.

Coding

Original protocol v1 preceded coding but followed initial discovery; it was not preregistered. One AI-assisted initial coder; no independent reliability estimate. Specificity, access coverage, claim type and actionability are distinct.

Interpretation

Quiet hours describe policy, not sound levels. Air conditioning does not prove individual control; adjustable lighting does not prove daylight exclusion. Brand and material claims do not establish guest usefulness. No composite score or citywide inference.

Selected background literature

Why these domains?

These observational hotel studies motivate environmental questions. They do not validate the disclosure rubric or establish which information guests find useful. Guest requests are an investigator-defined category. This is selected background, not a systematic review.

2021 / L1

Examining key hotel attributes for guest sleep and overall satisfaction

Cross-sectional, self-report survey of 609 frequent travelers.

Access: Abstract/metadata plus indexed sections; direct full text unavailable.

Recall, common-method and selection bias; association does not establish causality. Detailed coefficients not reused.

2020 / L2

Effects of Environmental Change on Travelers’ Sleep Health: Identifying Risk and Protective Factors

Self-report survey; 505 analyzed travelers at 28 hotels in Guangdong Province, China.

Access: Full text: methods and limitations reviewed.

Recall, exclusions and observational design limit causal and geographic inference. Narrative/table demographic discrepancies not propagated.

2018 / L3

“How’d you sleep?” measuring business travelers’ sleep quality and satisfaction in hotels

Self-report survey of 304 business travelers; at least two hotel nights on business in the past 30 days.

Access: Publisher abstract and metadata only; full text paywalled.

Only abstract methods assessed; fuller sampling, measures and analyses not verified.

Next: connect each domain to a guest decision, examine information-quality and content-analysis methods, and test comprehension separately.
